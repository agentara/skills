# Style DNA

Torn-paper editorial collage posters should feel like collectible visual evidence, not clean certificates, generic flyers, or presentation pages.

## Core Look

- Torn-paper editorial collage.
- Theme-specific evidence energy: news, launch, technology, product, person, culture, music, film, publication, exhibition, personal archive, or public event depending on the user request.
- Real person cutout integrated into paper layers when a person is provided.
- Huge type, oversized numbers, stamps, tape, rough labels.
- Readable event, evidence, or object data distributed as cards or stickers.
- High-impact, loud, slightly exaggerated, but still premium.
- Paper layers must dominate the composition: torn sheets, ripped windows, overlapping labels, tape, stamps, and rough photo edges should feel structural, not decorative.
- The person, group, product, object, or key visual should break through paper layers or sit as a cutout embedded in them. Do not leave the subject trapped inside a clean rectangular photo frame.

## Visual Materials

Use a dense mix of:

- torn paper edges
- old newspaper texture
- archive scraps
- news clippings
- launch notes
- ticket stubs or document fragments when relevant
- torn black paper fields
- cream paper slabs with rough fiber edges
- red, blue, yellow, lime, cyan, or hot pink tape strips
- crooked paper overlaps and drop shadows
- ripped photo windows, not clean photo rectangles
- evidence strips
- ID labels
- date stamps
- barcode
- safety pins
- paper tape
- ink splashes
- scuffs
- halftone dots
- photocopy grain
- risograph texture
- handwriting marks
- contour lines
- fragments that match the user theme, such as product detail, venue, backstage, archive, object texture, interface fragment, research note, city detail, publication clipping, stage lighting, lab note, or studio detail

## Color Directions

Default:

- energetic mid-bright paper collage with off-white / cream / warm paper / editorial red / ink black details
- electric lime, cyan, hot pink, or yellow as accent
- keep enough shadow and ink texture for depth, but do not make the overall poster dark, severe, or harshly high-contrast unless the user explicitly asks for that mood

Alternatives:

- blue-yellow launch impact for low-angle product or stage action
- orange-black editorial energy only when the user asks for a warmer or more severe direction
- black-white-red severe news or statement poster only when the user explicitly requests a severe, dark, or high-contrast direction

## Reference Discipline

If the user supplies mood or style images, treat them only as loose direction for energy, texture, camera angle, and typography density.

Never copy:

- exact layout
- exact slogan
- visible brand/logo
- unrelated subject matter
- watermark
- Chinese text from references

## Reject Checklist

Reject or redraw if the result:

- looks like the original certificate
- looks like a generic event flyer
- looks like a clean black dashboard with labels on top
- uses one large rectangular event photo as the main layout
- has only thin decorative torn borders around otherwise clean cards
- uses fake sponsor logos
- turns the person into a different person when a person reference was supplied
- invents a map, fake evidence, fake product evidence, fake UI, fake quote, fake statistic, or fake provenance
- adds impossible props or irrelevant symbolic objects
- makes the poster too clean, corporate, or presentation-like
- makes the poster overly dark, harsh, or high-contrast without explicit user direction
- treats torn paper as a border effect instead of the main material language
- hides the main data in unreadable microtext
- has weak theme energy
